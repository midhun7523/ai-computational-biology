# Career Pathways in AI & Computational Biology

AI & Computational Biology is a broad foundation. A student's eventual role depends on practical ability, project experience, internships, communication skills and sometimes postgraduate study.

## 1. Bioinformatics
**What it involves:** using computational methods to collect, compare, analyse and interpret biological data such as DNA, RNA and protein sequences.

**Skills to build:** Python or R, statistics, sequence analysis, Linux basics, data visualisation and biological interpretation.

**Portfolio idea:** analyse a small public sequence dataset and document the biological question, pipeline, results and limitations.

## 2. Computational biology research
**What it involves:** building or applying mathematical and computational models to understand biological processes.

**Skills to build:** mathematics, programming, statistics, modelling, scientific literature reading and reproducible research.

**Portfolio idea:** simulate a simple population or gene-expression model and explore how changing assumptions changes the results.

## 3. Biomedical data analysis
**What it involves:** preparing and analysing health-related, clinical, epidemiological or laboratory datasets.

**Skills to build:** Python/R, data cleaning, statistics, data visualisation, privacy awareness and careful interpretation.

**Portfolio idea:** analyse an open, de-identified public-health dataset and explain trends without claiming causation where the data do not support it.

## 4. AI and machine learning for life sciences
**What it involves:** applying machine learning to biological sequences, molecular data, biomedical images or other scientific datasets.

**Skills to build:** Python, linear algebra, probability, model evaluation, data leakage prevention, domain knowledge and validation.

**Portfolio idea:** compare a baseline model with a simple machine-learning model on a well-documented public dataset.

## 5. Biotechnology and pharmaceutical research support
**What it involves:** supporting experiments, analysing research data, helping prioritise hypotheses or developing computational tools for R&D teams.

**Skills to build:** molecular biology, statistics, data handling, scientific communication and awareness of experimental validation.

**Important:** computational predictions are not proof that a treatment works. Drug development requires extensive laboratory, preclinical and clinical validation.

## 6. Data science beyond biology
The programme's mathematics, statistics, algorithms and programming can also be relevant to broader data-science roles, if students develop the necessary general-purpose portfolio and role-specific skills.

## What employers may look for

- Demonstrable programming ability
- Strong quantitative foundations
- Clear reasoning and communication
- Experience cleaning and analysing real datasets
- Well-documented projects on GitHub
- Awareness of ethics, privacy, bias and reproducibility
- Ability to explain what a model can and cannot conclude

## A practical progression

1. **Foundations:** Python, statistics, algorithms and biology.
2. **Small analyses:** clean a dataset, make plots, explain what they show.
3. **Domain tools:** learn sequence analysis, scientific data formats or imaging workflows.
4. **Model responsibly:** use train/test separation, appropriate metrics and simple baselines.
5. **Research practice:** read papers, record sources, document limitations and seek feedback.
6. **Specialise:** choose a pathway based on the questions and projects you enjoy.

A degree can open directions; it does not automatically qualify someone for every role listed here. Check job descriptions and higher-study requirements for specific targets.
