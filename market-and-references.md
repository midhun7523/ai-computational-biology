# Market Context and References

This file separates external evidence from the repository's interpretation. Labour-market reports describe trends and employer expectations; they do not guarantee that a particular degree will lead to a specific job.

## What external sources indicate

### 1. AI and data skills are becoming more important
The World Economic Forum's *Future of Jobs Report 2025* identifies AI and big data among the fastest-growing skills expected by surveyed employers over 2025–2030. The report also emphasises analytical thinking, technological literacy and lifelong learning.

**What this means for AI & CB:** the computing and quantitative side of the programme is aligned with skills employers expect to become more important. It does not mean every AI-related role is secure or that demand is uniform across countries and industries.

Source: https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/

### 2. Computational biology is a recognised research area
The U.S. National Institute of General Medical Sciences describes bioinformatics and computational biology as combining biology with computer science, engineering, mathematics, biostatistics and physics. Its programme areas include bioinformatics, health informatics, infectious-disease modelling, multiscale modelling, biostatistics and scientific software.

**What this means for AI & CB:** the programme's interdisciplinary structure matches a recognised way of organising computational biomedical research.

Source: https://www.nigms.nih.gov/about/overview/BBCB/bioinfocomp/Pages/default

### 3. Biomedical research organisations are investing in AI and data science
The U.S. National Institutes of Health describes AI/ML applications across biomedical research, including omics, imaging, electronic health records and other biomedical data. Its resources also discuss responsible AI use and the importance of data quality, sharing and stewardship.

**What this means for AI & CB:** biological expertise and computational expertise can work together on real research problems, while privacy, validation and responsible use remain essential.

Sources:
- https://datascience.nih.gov/artificial-intelligence
- https://osp.od.nih.gov/policies/artificial-intelligence/
- https://beta.nih.gov/niams/about/niams-strategic-plan-fiscal-years-2025-2029/strategic-goals-and-priorities/research-priorities/priority-3

### 4. AI is being applied to life-sciences research
McKinsey's coverage of AI in life sciences describes applications in drug discovery and broader research workflows, while also noting that translating computational advances into reliable biological and clinical outcomes is challenging.

**What this means for AI & CB:** there is meaningful activity at the AI–biology intersection, but scientific validation is a real bottleneck. Students should learn both model-building and biological reasoning.

Sources:
- https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/mckinsey-explainers/ai-in-life-sciences-explained-the-technology-that-could-reinvent-medicine
- https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/life-sciences/our-insights/ai-drug-discovery-focusing-on-what-matters-most

## A balanced argument about jobs and AI

It is reasonable to say that AI & CB can help students build a differentiated skill set by combining computational capability with biological domain knowledge. It is **not** accurate to say the course is guaranteed to be “safe from AI,” that AI cannot replace work in biology, or that jobs and salaries are guaranteed to grow.

A defensible message is:

> AI will automate or change some tasks across many professions. Students who can understand biological questions, work with data, evaluate computational results and collaborate across disciplines may be better prepared to adapt. The advantage comes from continually building skills—not from being immune to change.

## Citation practice

When using statistics, market projections or claims about a company or research area:

- Link directly to the original report or official source.
- Include publication year and the population/geography covered.
- Distinguish employer expectations from observed hiring outcomes.
- Avoid presenting global projections as guaranteed Indian job outcomes.
- Update this document when the underlying sources change.
